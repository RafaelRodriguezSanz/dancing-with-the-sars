About

NISAR radar layers are products derived from real NISAR GCOV backscatter (HH, HV in dB) cut from NASA Earthdata: precomputed for Uruguay and processed on demand for any other area by the local server, scripts/serve.py. Alerts are candidates from simple rules, not confirmed events. The arrows skip dates without tiles or whose pass covers less than 95% of the box (still in the calendar, with a dashed outline when they have no products).

Made by Rafael Rodriguez Sanz, Montevideo, Uruguay, for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026, with AI assistance (Claude Code). Data: NISAR GCOV (NASA-ISRO) via NASA Earthdata / ASF DAAC, NASA CMR, NASA GIBS, NASA SRTM, Open-Meteo. Basemap, towns, roads and buildings © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL).

Other countries

This public site has Uruguay precomputed. Any other country or region (up to about 10° per side, e.g. Paraguay or Costa Rica) can be analyzed by running the app on your own computer with a free NASA Earthdata token:

  1. Get a token at urs.earthdata.nasa.gov (Generate Token).
  2. Clone the repository, copy .env.example to .env and paste the token there (never share it).
  3. Run make and open http://127.0.0.1:8000/site/
  4. Type the country in the search bar at the top and press Find, then Analyze area. Radar products, alerts and the flood map are built for it.
El Niño flood response

El Niño years bring heavy rain and rivers such as the Uruguay, Negro, Yí, Cuareim and Olimar overflow. It analyzes the selected area. The whole country answers instantly; select a riverside town (search it, draw a box or zoom in and press Use this view; about 30 km across) for streams, roads and a route to a dry refuge.

3.0 m

Planning aid, not an official plan: in an emergency follow SINAE and the departmental Cecoed. Simple model: water level spreads from rivers over terrain (NASA SRTM, ~30 m); it ignores levees, drains and one-way streets. Refuges are public buildings from OpenStreetMap, not confirmed shelters.
